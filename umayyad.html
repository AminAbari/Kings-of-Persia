Notes on the evidence

1 The Second Fitna (680–692), the civil war that followed Yazid I’s death and the massacre of Ali’s son Husayn at Karbala, ended Sufyanid rule and passed the caliphate to a cousin branch, the Marwanids, descended from Marwan I.

2 Umar II’s mother was a great-granddaughter of Caliph Umar ibn al-Khattab, giving him direct descent from a Rashidun caliph on both sides of his family. His brief, reforming reign was remembered so favorably by later Muslim historians that he is sometimes called “the fifth rightly-guided caliph.”

3 Yazid III’s mother, Shah Afrid, was said to be a granddaughter of the last Sasanian king, Yazdegerd III, taken captive during the conquest of Iran. Yazid reportedly boasted of uniting Arab and Persian royal blood — one of the few direct genealogical threads in this table connecting the Umayyads back to the pre-Islamic Persian throne.

The Umayyads fell to the Abbasid Revolution of 747–750, an uprising drawing heavily on Iranian (Khorasani) manpower under the general Abu Muslim. Marwan II was defeated at the Battle of the Great Zab in 750 and killed fleeing to Egypt; a surviving prince, Abd al-Rahman I, escaped to found a separate Umayyad emirate in Córdoba, Spain, with no further role in Iran’s history.
