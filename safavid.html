Notes on the evidence

1 Ismail I’s mother, Halima Begum, was a daughter of Uzun Hasan, the great Aq Qoyunlu ruler — meaning the founder of the explicitly Iranian, Shi’a Safavid state was himself descended, through his mother, from the Turkmen dynasty his movement had just overthrown.

2 Abbas I, the dynasty’s greatest ruler, moved the capital to Isfahan, whose Naqsh-e Jahan Square and surrounding architecture inspired the Persian saying “Isfahan is half the world.” He also broke the power of the Qizilbash Turkic tribal chiefs by creating a new corps of Caucasian ghulam slave-soldiers personally loyal to the shah.

3 Sultan Husayn’s weak, indecisive reign ended when Afghan Ghilzai rebels under Mahmud Hotak besieged and starved Isfahan into surrender in 1722, effectively ending Safavid power even though a shadow monarchy continued for another fourteen years.

4 Nader Khan Afshar, originally a general fighting to restore the Safavid pretenders Tahmasp II and the infant Abbas III, eventually deposed the last Safavid shah and crowned himself Nader Shah in 1736, founding the Afsharid dynasty and ending Safavid rule outright.
